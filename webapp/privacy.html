Privacy notice

Jun runs locally by default. Here is what is stored, which optional services receive data, and what stays under your control.

Version 2026-09-20 ≈3 min read

The short version

  • ✓ With the bundled local AI, Jun runs on your machine. Your account, chats, her memory and settings are stored in local files and your browser. A hosted AI provider is a separate choice described below.

Where your data lives

Jun OS is an unofficial fan project meant to be installed and run by you, on your own machine. Your account, your conversations, Jun’s memory notes and journal, the relationship gauges, the wardrobe and every setting are written to a database and a state directory on that machine and stay there. The app does not upload that stored data on its own.

The PHP application encrypts stored chat text, conversation titles and summaries, saved preferences, welcome messages, and Jun's memory files with a key for your account. Save the recovery code shown at signup or your first login after the encryption upgrade. It lets you reset a forgotten password; losing both password and code means a state backup alone cannot unlock encrypted content. Older accounts are encrypted when they log in, and interrupted encryption is retried on the next password login.

This does not encrypt all stored data: account details, timestamps, relationship and wardrobe state, logs, browser preferences and cached audio remain outside that protection. Running processes and the selected AI provider receive plaintext, and the browser stores a cookie containing the data key. Old backups may still contain unencrypted content. The separate Android client uses its own storage.

Whoever controls the machine controls the data: if you run Jun for other people, their chats are on your box and it is on you to keep it, and its backups, to yourself.

Other services this app can talk to

Whichever AI provider you pick is where your messages go. With the bundled Ollama or llama.cpp that is your own machine, and nothing leaves it. If you configure a hosted provider instead (OpenRouter is the one shipped today), your prompts are sent to that provider, and its terms and retention apply to them. Review those before enabling it. Voice and karaoke run locally in sidecar containers. A message beginning with /search sends the exact text after that prefix to DuckDuckGo once; Jun cannot add conversation or memory text to it. Karaoke can send song title, artist, album and duration to LRCLIB for a lyrics lookup.

Jun is an AI system

Jun is not a person and there is no person behind her. Her replies come from a language model, her voice from a text-to-speech model, and her expressions and poses from action tags the model writes into its own output. Nothing she says is a statement of fact about the world, about you, or about anyone else, and she has no feelings, memory or intentions beyond the notes and gauges this app stores for her. She is a fan-made character from a fictional game, not a real individual.

This is the disclosure required by art. 50(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act), which applies from 2 August 2026. If you run your own instance for anyone other than yourself, you are its deployer and the deployer duties in art. 50 are yours.

Generated audio carries provenance metadata marking it as artificially generated, as required by art. 50(2). Jun’s chat text is generated too, and stays on your machine. Assistive functions that only process material you supply (speech recognition on your microphone, karaoke stem separation on your own audio files) do not generate synthetic content and are not marked.

Automated decisions & children

Jun’s replies are generated by a language model, but nothing here makes automated decisions that have legal effects for you, or that similarly significantly affect you, within the meaning of art. 22.

This app displays adult content and is for people aged 18 or over. It is not intended for children. The current age gate is a self-declaration, not independent age verification or age assurance.
