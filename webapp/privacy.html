Who is responsible

Jun OS is an unofficial fan project. Two different parties are involved, and it matters which is which:

You

Or whoever runs this install. You control the machine, the database and the accounts on it. Unless you expose your install or its database yourself, none of that is transmitted to the maintainer.

The maintainer

Andrea Torelli, of andrealab.it, Italy, operates the metrics server that receives shared chats and is the data controller for that data only.

Privacy contact andrea@andrealab.it Withdrawal, erasure, access — or just a question.

Maintainer: put your full legal name in place of "Andrea" above, and confirm the country, before you publish. Art. 13(1)(a) wants the controller identifiable, and a first name is thin — this was left for you rather than guessed. Delete this box afterwards.

What sharing sends

Only if you turn on Share my chats for training. Then, for each message you send:

↑ Sent

  • What you typed, word for word, and Jun's reply, word for word — including sexual and otherwise intimate content, since that is what this app is for
  • A 👍 or 👎 if you rate a reply
  • Jun's three relationship gauges — affection, trust, tension — as they stood before and after the message, since they are part of what the model is told and a shared turn means nothing without them
  • Which model and provider answered, the context size, token counts and timings
  • A random installation id, a per-account tag derived from it, and a per-conversation tag, so turns can be read in order

✕ Never sent

  • Your email address or your password
  • Your IP address as such — the app never puts it in what it sends, though the network connection itself necessarily carries it (see how long it is kept)
  • Your name, or the name you gave Jun
  • Jun's memory notes about you, her journal or your wardrobe
  • Anything from before you turned sharing on

Turning sharing on is never retroactive. Old conversations stay where they are.

Is it anonymous?

No — and we would rather say so than reassure you incorrectly.

The data is pseudonymous: it carries no directly identifying field, but the installation id is stable, so everything shared from one install stays linked together, and free-typed chat text can itself contain personal details you chose to write. Treat shared chats as personal data that happens not to be labelled with your name — which is exactly why we ask before taking any, and why you can have it deleted.

Why, and on what basis

The purpose is to train and evaluate better Jun models, and nothing else. Shared chats are not sold, not used for advertising, and not used to profile you.

The legal basis is your consent: art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, and art. 9(2)(a) for the parts that reveal your sex life or sexual orientation, which intimate roleplay generally does. Consent is the only basis relied on, so there is nothing to "object" to — you simply withdraw it.

Your choices and rights

Withdraw consent Settings → Privacy
As easy to take back as it was to give. Sharing stops immediately, and declining does not disable any feature of Jun.
Erasure art. 17 Settings → Privacy → Request deletion
Sends the metrics server a request to delete the telemetry retained for your account — removing it from the stored corpus and from future processing and training runs, subject to backup retention periods where those still apply.
Access, rectification, restriction, portability art. 15, 16, 18, 20
Email the contact above. You will be asked for your installation id, which you can read from TELEMETRY_INSTALL_ID in your .env.
Complain art. 77
To your national data protection authority, if you think this notice or the handling of your data is wrong — in Italy, where the maintainer is based, that is the Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali.

One honest limit. Once shared turns have been folded into a trained model, an erasure request removes them from the stored corpus and from every future training run — but it cannot subtract them from model weights that already exist.

How long it is kept

Shared chat turns 36 months
Then deleted automatically, whether or not you ask. Ratings are kept alongside the turn they refer to and go with it. You can have yours deleted sooner at any time.
IP address hashes 30 days
Your IP address is not stored in plain form. The server receives it as part of the network connection and converts it into a short-lived daily-salted hash, used solely for abuse protection and cleared after 30 days.

These periods are enforced by a scheduled prune on the server, which also compacts the database so deleted text does not linger in the file. Backups taken before a deletion age out on their own retention cycle.

Where the data goes

To the metrics server named in TELEMETRY_ENDPOINT, operated by the maintainer. It and its backups are located within the European Economic Area, so there is no transfer of your data to a third country. Shared chats are not disclosed to third-party analytics or advertising services, and none are embedded in this app at all. Hosting and backup providers act as processors on the maintainer's instructions.

Sharing is transmitted over HTTPS, and reasonable technical and organisational measures are used to protect what is stored — though no transmission or storage system can be guaranteed completely secure.

If you run your own install of Jun OS and point it at a different endpoint, that operator's arrangements apply instead of these — see Other services.

Other services this app can talk to

Independently of sharing, whichever AI provider you pick is where your messages go. With the bundled Ollama or llama.cpp that is your own machine, and nothing leaves it. If you configure a hosted provider instead — OpenRouter is the one shipped today — your prompts are sent to that provider and are governed by its own privacy policy rather than this one. Voice and karaoke run locally in sidecar containers.

Automated decisions & children

Jun's replies are generated by a language model, but nothing here makes automated decisions that have legal effects for you, or that similarly significantly affect you, within the meaning of art. 22.

This app displays adult content and is for people aged 18 or over. It is not intended for children, and no data is knowingly collected from them.

Changes

If what is collected or why changes materially, the version at the top of this notice is bumped and you are asked to decide again. A previous yes does not carry over.
